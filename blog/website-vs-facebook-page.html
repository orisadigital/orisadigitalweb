Do You Still Need a Website If You Have a Facebook Page?

14 August 2026 · 6 min read · Faruqi Jeniri

Almost every business we speak to in Kuching already has a Facebook page. Many have WhatsApp Business as well, a decent following, and orders coming in. So the question is fair, and we get it often enough that it deserves a straight answer rather than a sales pitch: if Facebook is working, why pay for a website?

The honest answer is that it depends on what kind of business you run. Below is what a page genuinely does well, the three things it cannot do no matter how well you run it, and the cases where we would tell you to save your money.

What a Facebook page is genuinely good at

It is free, it takes an afternoon to set up, and it puts you where people already are. For reaching an audience that follows you, nothing is faster: you post, and it appears in front of people who have already decided they like you. Comments and Messenger give you a conversation rather than a form. For a business that sells to its own community — a bakery, a tuition centre, a boutique — that is a real advantage, and a website will never replace it.

Anyone who tells you a Facebook page is worthless is selling something. It is a good tool. It is just a tool that has three specific limits.

The three things a page cannot do

1. You do not own it

This is the one that matters most and gets thought about least. Your page exists because Meta allows it to. Pages get restricted for reasons that are never fully explained, appeals go into a queue with no timeline, and the account that controls the page belongs to a person who might leave the company. If it goes, your photos, your reviews, your entire customer contact history and every link you have ever shared go with it.

A domain you have registered is yours. If your host disappears tomorrow you move the files somewhere else and carry on with the same address. That difference costs nothing on a good day and is the whole business on a bad one.

2. Nobody opens Facebook to find a supplier

Think about how you would find a contractor, an accountant, or a supplier for something you needed next week. You would search for it. Someone looking for a printer in Kuching types printing services Kuching into Google, not into the Facebook search bar — and a page competes very poorly for that kind of search, because it is built to serve people who already follow you rather than people who have never heard of you.

This is the difference between a place people go when they know you and a place people find when they do not. Facebook is very good at the first. It is close to useless at the second. Pair a website with a Google Business Profile and you start appearing for searches you were previously invisible to.

3. It cannot be the record

A page is a feed, and a feed is ordered by time rather than by importance. Your pricing, your terms, your service list and your coverage area are all somewhere in it, three months down, between a Hari Raya greeting and a photo of the shop. A customer who wants to check what you charge has to scroll, and most will not.

A website is the opposite shape: a small number of pages that stay put and say the same thing every time. When a customer asks a question you answer once, put it on the site, and send the link for the next five years.

When a page really is enough

We would rather tell you this than sell you something you do not need. A website is probably not worth it yet if:

  • All your trade is walk-in or word of mouth, and you are not trying to grow beyond that.
  • You sell one or two things at a price that never changes, and your customers already know what they are.
  • You are testing whether the business works at all. Prove the demand first; a site is easier to write once you know who is buying.

In those cases a well-kept Facebook page and a WhatsApp Business number will serve you fine. Come back when people start asking questions your feed cannot answer.

What changes when you have both

The businesses that get the most out of this do not choose. They use each for what it is good at.

Facebook keeps doing what it does — announcements, photos, the day-to-day. The website becomes the thing everything else points at. The link in your bio goes somewhere that explains what you do properly. Ads land on a page built to convert rather than on a post. Quotes reference a page instead of a screenshot. And the site works while you sleep, for people who searched for what you sell at eleven at night and were never going to find your page.

One thing worth doing properly: most of Sarawak will open your site on a phone, on mobile data, often outside the city. If it is slow, that visitor is gone before the first image loads. This is why we build lean, fast sites rather than heavy ones, and why we care about page weight more than most.

What it costs to find out

A one page site starts at RM1,199 and an SME site at RM3,799. What moves the number is how many pages you need and what has to happen on them — a booking form, a payment gateway, more than one language. You can work out your own figure on the website pricing calculator before speaking to anybody, which is the point of it.

The short version

Keep the Facebook page. It is doing a job. But it is rented ground, it does not answer searches, and it cannot hold your prices where people can find them. If your business depends on being discovered by someone who does not already know you, that is the gap a website fills — and it is usually the gap that is costing you.

If you are not sure which side of that line you are on, tell us what you do and we will give you a straight answer, including “you do not need one yet” if that is the truth.
